01THE OPPORTUNITY

A useful local loop

Rescue what’s useful.
Make room for what’s next.

Fern & Cedar is a proposed creative reuse and materials rescue business for Bellingham and Whatcom County. It would gather usable craft supplies, commercial surplus, production offcuts, and other safe materials; sort and prepare them; then make them available again through affordable resale, bulk sharing, kits, or community programs.

01

Recover with care

Source materials from neighbors and businesses—with clear acceptance and safety rules.

02

Make reuse useful

Sort, describe, price, bundle, or match materials so others can actually use them.

03

Keep it community-rooted

Serve artists, educators, families, makers, and local organizations affordably.

Collection→Review & sort→Resale / share→Make again

RESEARCH-INFORMED POSITIONING

Complement what’s here.
Recover what’s missing.

The local landscape already includes valuable reuse organizations. The opportunity is a hypothesis to test with them and the community—not a claim that existing services fall short.

Local examples show several strong, specialized models: affordable art-supply reuse, textile diversion and creative space, and building-material salvage with business diversion partnerships. Fern & Cedar can explore a broader, active-sourcing model for cross-category surplus and materials matching—while coordinating rather than duplicating.

01

Build on relationships
Ask local organizations where collaboration or a different material stream could help.

02

Source with intention
Test scheduled business surplus and pickup only where logistics pencil out.

03

Reduce storage pressure
Match a material directly to a ready user when that beats warehousing it.

02ORGANIZATIONAL OPTIONS

Keep the decision open until the model is clearer

One mission.
Three possible structures.

The right choice depends on revenue, charitable activity, funding, risk, and who governs the work. Start by validating the operating model; get Washington legal and accounting advice before forming or fundraising.

OPTION A↗

Independent for-profit

An owner-operated company earns revenue from recovered-material sales, collection services, kits, and workshops.

Could fit when

Trading, sales, and flexible day-to-day operations are central to the first phase.

Plan for

Startup and working capital, records, insurance, permits, and applicable sales-tax collection and reporting.

Best to evaluate for a small trading pilot
OPTION B✳

Independent nonprofit

A separately governed charitable or educational organization centers public-benefit activities.

Could fit when

Community access and education are primary, with a board and a realistic funding plan.

Plan for

Formation and filings, independent governance, fundraising compliance, bookkeeping, and ongoing reporting.

Requires mission, governance & funding case
OPTION C◎

Legally separate hybrid

A business and a separate charitable initiative each do the work they are designed and authorized to do.

Could fit when

There is a proven business and a distinct, viable public-benefit program with its own leadership.

Plan for

Separate books, accounts, agreements, decision-making, conflict safeguards, and specialized legal advice.

Consider later, not as a shortcut

Important: A for-profit is not a charity. Material gifts to it are not automatically tax-deductible; don't promise deductions or mix charitable funds. Washington retail sales tax and reporting requirements also need review. See the IRS and Washington DOR sources below.

MICRO-LAUNCH · FIRST MATERIALS
SORT SMALL.
LEARN A LOT.

START WITH MATERIALS ALREADY IN HAND

Prove the loop
before the lease.

Start with a small, permission-cleared batch of existing materials. Track what it takes to assess, store, prepare, price, and move each category before adding pickups, a warehouse, or opening hours.

  • 01
    Choose a narrow batch

    Use items you own or have clear permission to use; begin with low-risk categories.

  • 02
    Track the real work

    Record time, storage footprint, prep cost, sales, and materials that don't move.

  • 03
    Adjust with local partners

    Ask what overlaps, what is missing, and where a direct material match could help.

Turn this into a launch plan →
03FROM IDEA TO FIRST SEASON

A low-cost, decision-gated launch

Test the loop.
Then scale what works.

Keep the first phase small enough to learn. A storefront, staff, broad material acceptance, and regular pickups come later—only if the pilot supports them.
